# Analysis Frameworks
 
## Cognitive Biases to Name
 
When delivering a reality check, identify which of these biases is distorting the user's thinking. Name it explicitly and explain how it applies.
 
### Most Common in Career Decisions
 
| Bias | What It Does | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Dunning-Kruger** | Overestimating competence in areas of low experience | "I can pick up ML in 3 months and land a senior role" |
| **Survivorship Bias** | Seeing only winners, ignoring the 95% who failed the same path | "My friend pivoted to tech and doubled their salary" |
| **Sunk Cost Fallacy** | Sticking with a career path because of years already invested | "I have 8 years in this field, I can't switch now" |
| **Anchoring** | Fixating on one data point (usually salary) and ignoring total comp, growth, stability | "This role pays $200k" (ignoring it's a 2-person startup with 6 months runway) |
| **Optimism Bias** | Believing you'll be the exception to statistical base rates | "Most bootcamp grads struggle, but I'm different" |
| **Status Quo Bias** | Staying in a bad situation because change feels riskier than it is | "At least I have a job" when the industry is dying |
| **Availability Heuristic** | Overweighting recent/vivid examples over base rates | "AI is replacing all developers" based on 3 Twitter threads |
 
### Most Common in Business/Startup Decisions
 
| Bias | What It Does | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Confirmation Bias** | Seeking evidence that supports the idea, ignoring evidence against | "10 friends said they'd use it" (none would pay) |
| **IKEA Effect** | Overvaluing something because you built it | "My MVP is better than competitors" (it's not) |
| **Planning Fallacy** | Underestimating time, cost, and complexity by 2-3x | "We'll launch in 3 months" (it takes 12) |
| **Narrative Fallacy** | Constructing a compelling story that ignores inconvenient data | "Uber for X" (ignoring why Uber specifically works and X doesn't) |
| **Bandwagon Effect** | Pursuing an idea because it's trendy, not because fundamentals work | "AI wrapper" businesses in 2024-2026 |
| **Neglect of Probability** | Ignoring base rates of startup failure (90%+ fail) | "We're different because we have passion" |
| **Endowment Effect** | Overvaluing your idea/equity because it's yours | Rejecting $500k acquisition when company is worth $200k |
 
## Career Analysis Framework
 
### The 5-Factor Career Assessment
 
1. **Market Demand Score (search for data)**
   - Job postings trend (growing/shrinking over 12-24 months)
   - Ratio of applicants to openings (where data exists)
   - Salary trajectory for this role (BLS, Glassdoor, Levels.fyi)
   - Geographic concentration vs. remote availability
2. **Candidate Fit Score (assess honestly)**
   - Years of directly relevant experience vs. job requirements
   - Skills match: hard skills, tools, certifications
   - Portfolio/proof of work quality vs. top 25% of applicants
   - Network strength in target industry/company
3. **Timing Score (research current conditions)**
   - Current hiring cycle (Q1 ramp-up vs. Q4 freeze)
   - Industry-specific conditions (layoffs, growth, funding)
   - Macroeconomic indicators affecting the sector
   - AI/automation risk timeline for this role
4. **Opportunity Cost Score (calculate)**
   - Current compensation vs. expected compensation during transition
   - Time investment (months of prep/search)
   - Skills atrophy in current role during transition
   - Alternative paths with better risk-adjusted returns
5. **5-Year Trajectory (project forward)**
   - Where does the median person in this role end up?
   - What's the ceiling? How many reach it?
   - What adjacent roles open up? What closes off?
   - Industry longevity — will this role exist in 5 years?
## Startup/Business Analysis Framework
 
### The 7-Point Viability Test
 
1. **Problem Severity (1-10)**
   - Is this a hair-on-fire problem or a nice-to-have?
   - How much money/time does the target customer currently waste on this?
   - What's the current workaround, and how painful is it?
2. **Market Size (with receipts)**
   - TAM: Total addressable market (cite the source)
   - SAM: Serviceable addressable market (your realistic slice)
   - SOM: Serviceable obtainable market in years 1-3
   - Red flag: If you need to "educate the market," the market doesn't exist yet
3. **Competitive Moat (or lack thereof)**
   - Who are the top 5 competitors? (Search Crunchbase, Product Hunt, G2)
   - What's their funding? Revenue? Growth rate?
   - Why would a customer switch FROM them TO you?
   - How long until a well-funded competitor copies your differentiator?
4. **Unit Economics (do the math)**
   - Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) — realistic, not optimistic
   - Lifetime Value (LTV) — based on comparable churn rates
   - LTV:CAC ratio (need >3:1 for viable business)
   - Gross margin after all variable costs
   - Months to payback on customer acquisition
5. **Founder-Market Fit**
   - Does the founder have domain expertise or network in this space?
   - Have they sold to this customer before?
   - Do they have an unfair distribution advantage?
   - Can they build v1 themselves or do they need to hire?
6. **Timing Evidence**
   - What changed recently that makes this possible/necessary NOW?
   - Is there a regulatory, technological, or behavioral shift?
   - Search for "why now" — if there's no clear answer, timing may be wrong
7. **The Wallet Test**
   - Have people paid for this (not just said they would)?
   - Pre-orders, LOIs, signed contracts — what's the hardest evidence?
   - "Would you use this?" is worthless. "Will you pay $X/month starting today?" is the only question.
## General Decision Framework
 
### Pre-Mortem Analysis
 
Before evaluating, imagine it's 12 months later and the decision failed spectacularly. Ask:
- What went wrong? (List the 3 most likely failure modes)
- What early warning signs were ignored?
- What assumption turned out to be false?
- What external factor changed?
### The Regret Minimization Test
 
- If you do this and it fails: what's the worst realistic outcome? Can you recover?
- If you don't do this and it succeeds without you: how much regret?
- Which regret is more recoverable?
### Second-Order Effects
 
Most people only think one step ahead. Force second-order thinking:
- "If I take this job, then _____, which means _____, which leads to _____"
- Map at least 3 levels deep on both the optimistic and pessimistic branches
